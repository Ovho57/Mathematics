# 03 — Continuity

A function is continuous at (x=a) if:
1. (f(a)) is defined.
2. (lim_{x\to a}f(x)) exists.
3. The limit equals (f(a)).

Informally, the graph has no break at that point.

## Types of discontinuity

- **Removable:** a hole that could be filled by defining the missing value.
- **Jump:** left and right limits differ.
- **Infinite:** function values grow without bound near a point.

## Why continuity matters

The Intermediate Value Theorem says that a continuous function that moves from below a value (k) to above (k) must equal (k) somewhere in between.

## Example

(f(x)=x^2) is continuous at (2):
\[
\lim_{x\to2}x^2=4=f(2)
\]

## Practice

Is (f(x)=1/x) continuous at (x=0)?

**Answer:** No. It is undefined there and has no finite two-sided limit.
