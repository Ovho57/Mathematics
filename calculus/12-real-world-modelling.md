# 12 — Real-World Calculus Modelling

Word problems test whether you can translate a situation into mathematics.

## The modelling method

1. Read carefully; identify what is being asked.
2. Define variables and units.
3. List known values, constraints, and conditions.
4. Choose a model: function, derivative, integral, or differential equation.
5. Build the equation from the story.
6. Solve it.
7. Interpret the result in context.
8. Check units, signs, domain, and whether the answer is reasonable.

## Example: motion

Position:
\[
s(t)=t^2+3t
\]
where (s) is metres and (t) seconds.

Velocity:
\[
v(t)=s'(t)=2t+3
\]

At (t=4):
\[
v(4)=11\text{ m/s}
\]

Interpretation: position is increasing at 11 metres per second at that instant.

## Marginal cost

If total cost is (C(q)), then (C'(q)) represents marginal cost: approximately the extra cost for one more unit near (q). It is a local approximation, not necessarily the exact cost.

## Rate and accumulation

If (r(t)) is net inflow in litres/minute:
\[
\int_a^b r(t)dt
\]
gives the change in water volume. Negative rate means the quantity decreases.

## Common wording clues

- “instantaneous rate”, “gradient at” → derivative
- “maximum”, “minimum”, “optimum” → optimization
- “total”, “accumulated”, “area” → integral
- “rate proportional to amount” → differential equation

These are clues, not automatic answers; read the full problem.

## Practice

Position is (s(t)=5t^2) metres. Find velocity at (t=3) seconds.

**Solution:** (v(t)=10t), so (v(3)=30\text{ m/s}).
