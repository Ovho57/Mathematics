# 13 — Exam Strategy

## A reliable solution structure

1. State the formula or method.
2. Substitute the known values.
3. Show algebra and calculus steps.
4. State the final answer clearly.
5. Include units and interpretation for applied problems.

## Recognizing question types

- Gradient at a point: differentiate and substitute.
- Tangent: derivative gives slope; use point-slope form.
- Stationary points: solve (f'(x)=0), then classify.
- Area: identify bounds and integrate.
- Total change from a rate: definite integral.
- Unknown constant: use initial/boundary condition.

## Common exam traps

- Forgetting (+C) for indefinite integrals.
- Forgetting to adjust bounds after substitution.
- Using degrees when trig calculus expects radians.
- Finding stationary points but ignoring the requested interval.
- Confusing signed integral with geometric area.
- Forgetting units.
- Assuming every stationary point is a maximum/minimum.
- Incorrect product or chain rule.

## Time management

Start with questions you can solve. If stuck, write relevant information and move on temporarily. Reserve time to check arithmetic, signs, endpoints, and units.

## Learn from errors

Label each mistake: concept, method, algebra, arithmetic, misreading, or missing interpretation. Reattempt it later without viewing the solution.
