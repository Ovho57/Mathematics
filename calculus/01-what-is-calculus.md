# 01 — What Is Calculus?

Calculus studies **change** and **accumulation**.

Imagine a car. Its position tells you where it is. Average speed describes how position changes over a time interval. Instantaneous speed describes how quickly position changes at one exact moment.

## Differential calculus: change

The derivative measures instantaneous change. Examples include speed, population growth, graph steepness, and optimization.

## Integral calculus: accumulation

The integral adds many small contributions. Examples include distance from speed, total water flowing through a pipe, area, and total cost from a marginal-cost function.

## Average rate of change

For (f(x)) from (a) to (b):
\[
\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}
\]

Example (f(x)=x^2) from (1) to (3):
\[
\frac{9-1}{3-1}=4
\]

This is the slope of the secant line. Shrinking the interval toward one point leads to the derivative.

## Accumulation idea

Imagine splitting a region into many tiny strips and adding their areas. Making the strips thinner leads to the definite integral.
